Reinforcement Learning for Antenna Design: A Deep Dive

Introduction

Millimeter-wave antenna design is a computationally taxing engineering challenge. At frequencies near 28 GHz, even minute geometry variations can impact performance metrics like impedance matching and radiation efficiency. Traditional solvers like CST are accurate but slow, making extensive optimization resource-intensive.

This post explores an uncertainty-aware Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework that reduces electromagnetic simulation costs by 65%. By integrating surrogate models with Soft Actor-Critic agents, we automate geometry design while ensuring fabrication feasibility.

Why Reinforcement Learning?

RL offers a gradient-free alternative where an agent learns to improve geometry through environmental interaction. To solve high evaluation requirements, we replace direct solver calls with learned surrogate approximations that account for prediction uncertainty.

System Architecture

The optimization pipeline consists of four integrated components:

  • High-fidelity CST simulations for ground-truth data.
  • Blended surrogate models for fast, uncertainty-aware predictions.
  • A fabrication-aware RL environment.
  • An active learning loop for continuous model improvement.

Blended Surrogate Models

The framework utilizes an ensemble—including LightGBM regressors and a Multilayer Perceptron—to predict metrics like resonant frequency and reflection coefficient. Ensemble disagreement provides an uncertainty signal, preventing the agent from exploiting unreliable design regions.

RL Environment and Reward Design

The agent proposes design perturbations within realistic PCB fabrication boundaries. The SAC algorithm is used for stability. The uncertainty-aware reward penalizes surrogate "blind spots" to prioritize reliable designs.

Case Study: 28 GHz FR-4 Antenna

The framework was tested on a rectangular microstrip patch antenna optimized for 28 GHz. The final design achieved a resonant frequency of 28.12 GHz, a minimum reflection coefficient of -52.2 dB, and a fractional bandwidth of 8.93%.

Key Takeaways

  • RL enables gradient-free antenna geometry optimization.
  • Blended surrogates significantly lower EM simulation overhead.
  • Uncertainty-aware rewards ensure manufacturability.
  • The framework applies to multi-band antennas and phased arrays.
